San Francisco street parking

How long can the car stay?

Each curb is colored by how long you can stay if you park at the time below. A stay ends at the first street sweeping, time limit or other restriction, and never later than the 72-hour limit.

    What this map can't see

    • Posted signs always win. Some regulation records were last edited in 2017, and the city says the regulations data hasn't been fully vetted.
    • Meters: a curb with any active meter is treated as metered every day 9 AM–6 PM. DataSF's meter list is current, but its schedules date from 2014, so check the meter for its real hours and limits.
    • Time-limit records are patchy on commercial streets (in the Sunset, only 9 of 60 Taraval block sides have one). Expect posted limits the map doesn't show.
    • Tow-away lanes (commute-hour no stopping) are mostly missing from the data. Downtown and on arterials, read every sign.
    • Color curbs (red, yellow, white, blue, green), bus zones, driveways, hydrants and the clearance needed near crosswalks and corners are not in the data. The map shows which block sides you can use, not exact legal spaces.
    • Temporary restrictions are not included: construction, events, film permits, or Great Highway or other street closures.
    • Bike-lane sweeping isn't in the sweeping data.
    • Holiday exceptions to sweeping are ignored, so the map assumes sweeping always happens.
    • Times are local wall-clock time. A stay that spans a daylight-saving change can be off by an hour.
    • The 72-hour rule (CVC 22651(k)) applies even with a disabled placard or RPP permit, and it's enforced when someone complains. After moving, SFMTA says to go at least one block or one-tenth of a mile.
    • No line means the street isn't in either dataset. Check the posted signs.
    • The sweeping data is missing many streets in Russian Hill, North Beach and Nob Hill. Curbs there show as dashed regulation-only lines and may well be swept.
    • Dashed lines are regulations with no matching sweeping row. The map treats them as unswept, which may be wrong.
